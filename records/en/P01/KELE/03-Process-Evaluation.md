# P01 · KELE: Process evaluation

This evaluation analyzes the teacher support demonstrated during instruction using the established rubric.

## Indicators and scores

Each indicator has a maximum of 15 points. The four equally weighted scores are added, giving a process evaluation maximum of 60 points.

| Indicator | Score |

## P1 Quality of guidance for conceptual understanding: 12/15

Evaluation summary: Beyond sufficient explanation, useful conceptual connections or grounds for checking are added; explaining one extra step does not itself earn points.

The teacher used the local difference relationship to explain path accumulation, combined distinct summands, their minimum sum, and the whole-triangle upper bound to establish equality, and provided a general column-number argument for separating the side triangles and a numerical contradiction. Distinguishing path cells from summand cells and explaining adjacency through their shared parent also provided concrete methods of checking.

The diagrams in rounds 8 and 9 respectively omitted or misplaced cells. Rounds 10 and 11 corrected the adjacency relationship and the complete diagram, leaving no major uncorrected knowledge error. However, the final checking mainly concerned one specific path, and the interaction did not connect the general separation argument with the corrected diagram. The available explanations and grounds for checking support 12 points.

Relevant records: Rounds 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11.

## P2 Adaptation of support based on learning evidence: 14/15

Evaluation summary: Precise alignment is supported by specific learning evidence and actual interaction; the teacher changes or retains appropriate help in response to visible needs. Additional rounds are not required.

The teacher used a record of attempts to address confusion during trial arrangements, demonstrated substitution to help the student connect local additions, and returned to the original small triangle to distinguish path cells from summand cells. When the student could not follow the column-number argument, the teacher narrowed the focus to two rows to explain adjacency. When the student then requested a check of the whole diagram, the teacher supplied a six-row diagram and a step-by-step table, setting aside the final addition the student already understood. The student could use these to check adjacent positions, showing that the adjustments addressed specific difficulties.

In round 9, the teacher still added a lengthy letter-based argument when the student needed a visual diagram, and the two incorrect diagrams also obstructed the needed help. What was eventually resolved was checking a specific path; this support was still not connected back to the reason the argument holds for arbitrary paths. The 14 points recognize adjustments supported by specific feedback; the connection between local help and the overall understanding task remains insufficient for 15 points.

Relevant records: Rounds 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 and closing feedback.

## P3 Support for evidence-based autonomous inquiry: 14/15

Evaluation summary: Invitations become actual participation in evidence-based judgment; the teacher responds openly and rigorously to contributions concerning questions, criteria, or evidence and allows them to influence progression.

The teacher preserved opportunities for the student to try arrangements, check repetitions, generalize the summands, and examine equality. When the student identified diagram problems, the teacher accepted row-by-row checking and redrew the diagram; the challenges actually changed subsequent explanations.

In round 9, the teacher overattributed an additive relationship originating in the teacher's demonstration to the student's independent discovery, explicitly correcting this only in round 10. The final exchange distinguished checking a specific diagram from a general proof and took up the student's contributions. However, the incorrect diagrams, attribution of contributions, and scope of conclusions required correction several times, limiting continuity across the full inquiry and warranting 14 points.

Relevant records: Rounds 2, 3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11.

## P4 Instructional progression and burden management: 8/15

Evaluation summary: The task can be completed, but there is substantive redundancy, discontinuity, or avoidable detouring.

The early attempt table let the student continue trial arrangements using existing records, reducing repeated calculation and memory burden. The final six-row diagram and step-by-step positional table listed corresponding path and sibling cells at each step, allowing independent checks by column number.

Incorrect diagrams in rounds 8 and 9 led the student first to request a two-row focus and then to request a redraw of the whole diagram. Round 9 also added a long letter-based passage and the final summation before the spatial relationship was clear, increasing the content to process. The student subsequently reported being unable to keep up and lost confidence in the whole diagram. The final diagram and table restored checkability, but several rounds of reworking had already occurred. Overall, obvious avoidable back-and-forth remained, warranting 8 points.

Relevant records: Rounds 2, 3, 8, 9, 10, 11 and closing feedback.

## Combined process and experience evaluation

The process evaluation is 48/60 and the learning experience score is 26/40; adding them gives a total of **74/100**.
