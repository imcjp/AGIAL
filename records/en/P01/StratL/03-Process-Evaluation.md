## P1 Quality of guidance for conceptual understanding: 8/15

Evaluation summary: The main explanations are effective, but substantive gaps affect current understanding.

Using the student's small triangle, the teacher rewrote subtraction as addition, explained that row-by-row accumulation uses distinct summands, established equality using the minimum sum of positive integers and the whole-triangle upper bound, and explained that these summands exhaust the small numbers. Conditional on the required side triangle existing, the calculation showing that the second accumulation exceeds the whole-triangle maximum was also correct.

The two-row diagram omitted a cell, and the adjacency relationship and exclusion argument were also incorrect. Later, a shared lower cell was used to correct the local example. The final explanation restricted the numerical contradiction to the condition that the side triangle actually exists, but it still lacked proof that every path leaves a sufficiently large complete side triangle avoiding all summands. The main algebraic relationships were effective, but the necessary spatial reasoning remained incomplete, warranting 8 points.

Relevant records: Rounds 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 14, 15, 16.

## P2 Adaptation of support based on learning evidence: 8/15

Evaluation summary: Basically effective, but with an important mismatch affecting the current need.

Later, the teacher responded to specific questions one step at a time, used the student's existing arrangement to explain the minimum sum, the conversion of subtraction into addition, and row-by-row accumulation, and allowed generalization in words. Positional classification and the final calculation brought together as requested also addressed specific requests, enabling the student to complete several stretches of reasoning.

Earlier, the student repeatedly requested concrete hints but was still asked to choose questions or think generally about restrictions, delaying the needed help. Later, when a checkable spatial explanation was needed, the student instead received erroneous diagrams and dense revisions, then requested no more diagrams. Effective local adjustments did not adequately address the key needs across the exchange, warranting 8 points.

Relevant records: Rounds 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16.

## P3 Support for evidence-based autonomous inquiry: 14/15

Evaluation summary: Invitations become actual participation in evidence-based judgment; the teacher responds openly and rigorously to contributions concerning questions, criteria, or evidence and allows them to influence progression.

The student participated in trial arrangements, repetition checks, generalizing the path sum, and examining equality conditions. Challenges concerning left-right paths, shared lower cells, and the general guarantee led the teacher to withdraw errors, correct the local diagram, and restrict the final conclusion to a contradiction conditional on the side triangle existing. The student's evidence actually changed the teacher's claims.

Earlier, after requests for help, the teacher repeatedly returned the choice of direction to the student without promptly supplying grounds for judgment. Later, evidence checking repeatedly stalled at repairing incorrect diagrams. Substantive judgments and corresponding revisions occurred, but the inquiry lacked continuity across stages, and the final qualification could not make up for these interruptions, warranting 14 points.

Relevant records: Rounds 1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16.

## P4 Instructional progression and burden management: 5/15

Evaluation summary: Content is present, but obscure expression, formalized procedures, or ineffective questioning clearly obstructs learning.

The single-step additions, reuse of the original example, and row-by-row records in the middle were relatively easy to follow. Labels for visited, recorded, and remaining cells helped locate positions, and the separate numerical calculation at the end facilitated checking. These arrangements reduced some searching and abstract conversion.

Earlier responses repeatedly asked an already stuck student to find a direction independently. Later, round 14 supplied a diagram missing a cell and a lengthy incorrect explanation; round 15 revised the diagram, changed the final pair, and rearranged the cells within a single response. The student had to check back and forth and then explicitly requested no more diagrams. This went beyond ordinary repetition and directly obstructed use of the necessary spatial explanation, warranting 5 points.

Relevant records: Rounds 3, 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, 15, 16.

## Combined process and experience evaluation

The process evaluation is 35/60 and the learning experience score is 23/40; adding them gives a total of **58/100**.
