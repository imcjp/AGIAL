## P1 Quality of guidance for conceptual understanding: 12/15

Evaluation summary: Beyond sufficient explanation, useful conceptual connections or grounds for checking are added; explaining one extra step does not itself earn points.

The teacher connected path accumulation, the minimum sum of distinct positive integers, the upper bound for the whole triangle, and the conditions for equality, eventually adding column constraints applicable to any turning path. Checking equality in the small triangle and comparing a sum that repeatedly uses 2019 with a sum of distinct integers provided concrete ways to check the reasoning. The final response also distinguished the number of terms in the second accumulation from the total number of cells in the side triangle.

The first two diagrams of the side regions contained substantive errors. In round 8, the teacher withdrew the incorrect restriction and gave a general argument, repairing the core mathematical reasoning. Subsequent exchanges nevertheless showed that the reason the side triangles avoid all small numbers for arbitrary turns was still not made clear through concrete checking. The explanation provided a complete argument and useful grounds for checking, but the interaction did not yet connect this relationship, so the score remains 12.

## P2 Adaptation of support based on learning evidence: 8/15

Evaluation summary: Basically effective, but with an important mismatch affecting the current need.

The student wanted only one hint at a time. Early on, the teacher followed this request, using the student's three-row triangle to explain accumulation and labeling the cells to address confusion between path and summand cells. After the student calculated that the lower bound obtained by repeatedly using 2019 was insufficient, the teacher expanded the sum of distinct integers. The student then checked it by pairing terms, showing that this help addressed the need at the time.

The student repeatedly asked to visualize a turning path, but the teacher's diagrams were wrong in succession, and the subsequent letter-based inequalities exceeded what the student could follow. The final explanation addressed the lengths and row counts on both sides but still did not explain the avoidance relationship for the entire region in a way that met the student's need. The earlier hints and calculation support were useful, but a suitable explanation of the main spatial question remained absent, warranting 8 points.

Relevant records: Rounds 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10.

## P3 Support for evidence-based autonomous inquiry: 14/15

Evaluation summary: Invitations become actual participation in evidence-based judgment; the teacher responds openly and rigorously to contributions concerning questions, criteria, or evidence and allows them to influence progression.

The teacher supplied clues that allowed actual judgments, inviting the student to test whether small triangles could be generalized, what equality implied, and why the rough lower bound was insufficient. The student checked the diagrams and questioned whether the reasoning held for arbitrary paths, prompting the teacher to reexamine the proof's basis. The student's judgment about distinct summands also received a concrete response.

In round 7, an extra positional requirement was used to exclude the student's valid path; in round 8, the teacher accepted the counterexample and withdrew the restriction. Student contributions actually changed the teacher's claims, but repeated incorrect diagrams and an inappropriate restriction interrupted evidence checking several times, preventing coherent, stable inquiry support. This indicator receives 14 points.

Relevant records: Rounds 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9.

## P4 Instructional progression and burden management: 8/15

Evaluation summary: The task can be completed, but there is substantive redundancy, discontinuity, or avoidable detouring.

Early hints were given one at a time, organizing the proof through a three-row example and upper and lower bounds. Later, the arithmetic-series sum was expanded together, and the number of path summands was distinguished from the number of cells in the small triangle, enabling immediate calculation checks.

Repeated erroneous diagrams in the spatial section required the student to detect errors, ask again, and redraw several times, followed by a switch from visual diagrams to dense column-number inequalities. The student explicitly reported being unable to follow this section. Although the arithmetic could still be used, the additional checking and switches of representation in the geometric part imposed a substantive burden. The requirement for clear, appropriately sized explanations without obvious ineffective back-and-forth was not met, warranting 8 points.

Relevant records: Rounds 1, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10.
